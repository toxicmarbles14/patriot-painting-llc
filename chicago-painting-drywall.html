Residential Painting

Spaces Worth Refreshing

Living Rooms

Warm neutrals, soft whites, and accent colors can create welcoming gathering spaces. Consistent wall finishes help the room feel polished.

Kitchens & Dining Rooms

Busy household spaces benefit from finishes selected for their appearance and cleanability. Proper preparation is especially important around frequently used areas.

Bedrooms

Hallways & Staircases

These high-traffic spaces often develop scuffs, nail holes, and dents. Repairing imperfections before painting can improve the overall finish.

Choosing Paint

Understanding Different Finishes

Flat and Matte

Low-sheen finishes can make minor imperfections less noticeable and are commonly considered for ceilings and quieter spaces.

Eggshell

A subtle sheen makes eggshell a versatile option for many residential walls.

Satin

Satin finishes offer a more noticeable sheen and can be useful in frequently occupied rooms.

Semi-Gloss

Often chosen for doors and trim, semi-gloss creates a more reflective surface that highlights architectural details.

Surface Preparation

Why Drywall Matters Before Painting

Even a carefully selected paint color cannot completely hide poorly prepared drywall. Raised seams, dents, rough patches, and uneven repairs can remain visible after the final coat.

Preparing drywall may involve patching small holes, reinforcing damaged joints, sanding compound, and priming repaired surfaces.

In older Chicago buildings, identifying whether the surface is drywall or plaster is particularly important. The correct repair method depends on the existing material and the cause of damage.

Learn more about these methods in our Chicago drywall repair guide.

Project Planning

How to Prepare for a Painting Project

Homeowners can make a painting project easier to organize by considering which rooms need attention, whether existing walls require repairs, and what colors or finishes will suit each space.

It is also helpful to think about lighting. Natural daylight and artificial lighting can make the same paint color appear different throughout the day.

Testing a small sample before committing to a full room can help clarify how the color will look in the intended environment.
