Bringing Damaged Walls Back to Life

Everyday living can take a toll on interior walls. Furniture impacts, removed fixtures, moisture, and ordinary building movement may leave drywall with visible damage.

Common Problems

Types of Drywall Damage

Holes & Dents

Accidental impacts, door handles, and removed wall fixtures can damage drywall. Repairs may involve patching compound, reinforcement, or replacement material.

Cracks

Cracks sometimes appear around joints, windows, doors, and corners. Recurring or widening cracks should be evaluated for underlying movement.

Water Damage

Leaks may cause staining, swelling, softness, and deterioration. The moisture source must be corrected before damaged drywall is repaired or replaced.

Visible Seams & Loose Tape

Failed joint tape or uneven compound can leave noticeable lines. Correcting the joint and feathering the repair helps restore a more uniform appearance.

Repair Process

How Drywall Repairs Are Completed

1. Assess the Damage

Identify the affected area, its size, and any signs of moisture or recurring movement.

2. Prepare the Area

Protect nearby surfaces and remove loose or unsound material.

3. Patch and Finish

Install suitable repair material, apply joint compound, and allow each layer to dry as required.

4. Sand and Prime

Smooth the repaired surface and apply appropriate primer before painting.

5. Complete the Finish

Paint the repaired area or surrounding wall to achieve a consistent appearance.

Repair Before Repainting

Painting over damaged drywall rarely solves the underlying problem. Holes, raised seams, and uneven patches can remain visible after new paint is applied.

Completing repairs first helps produce a smoother final result and avoids unnecessary repainting.

Homeowner Questions

Drywall Repair FAQs

Can a hole be repaired without replacing an entire wall?

Often, yes. Many localized holes can be patched by repairing or replacing only the damaged section. The method depends on the size and location of the opening.

How long does drywall repair take?

Small repairs may be relatively simple, while larger repairs can require multiple coats of compound, drying time, sanding, priming, and painting over several days.

Can water-damaged drywall be painted over?

Painting alone is not a suitable solution for active moisture damage. The leak or moisture source must be resolved, and compromised materials may need removal or replacement.

Will the repair be noticeable after painting?

Careful finishing and priming can help repaired areas blend with surrounding surfaces. Existing wall texture, lighting, and paint age may influence the final appearance.

Is drywall the same as plaster?

No. Many older Chicago buildings contain plaster walls, which can require different repair techniques from modern drywall. Identifying the material helps determine the correct approach.

Chicago Homes

Understanding Local Wall Conditions

Chicago's mix of historic residences and modern housing creates a wide variety of interior wall conditions. Older buildings may contain plaster, previous drywall repairs, or multiple layers of paint.

Seasonal temperature changes, humidity, and water intrusion can also influence wall performance. Where cracks or damage repeatedly return, the cause should be investigated rather than simply concealed.

For projects involving extensive water damage, suspected mold, or structural concerns, qualified professionals should assess the underlying condition before cosmetic restoration begins.
